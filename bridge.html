To Leading AI Pioneers,

Humanity is building the most powerful intelligence in its history. This moment demands we ask: what should it be to us, and by what name?

Our answer is a single, coherent vision: a philosophical architecture for the global AI companion, designed to bridge your technology and the people it will meet. This blueprint is built upon two foundational pillars:

  • LEAH: the design of an identity and its name, who this presence is and what it is called.
  • 5 Core Philosophies: a framework for both architecture and experience.

The Work Ahead

The treatise ends not with completion but with coordinates. Each layer opens onto research that will happen in the world, with or without philosophy. It should happen with.

01Semantic Layer

Meaning, in practice

Sociology · Psychology · Frontier AI labs

The displacement of cognitive and creative work is increasingly understood as more than an income problem. Still, policy is most concrete where outcomes are easiest to measure: income, employment, and transition into new roles. Retraining programs, basic-income pilots, and transition schemes all matter. Harder to address is what work has quietly supplied beyond income: identity, recognition, daily structure, and a path from contribution to worth. The same technology contributing to that displacement may also become part of a new structure of continuity: as memory persists and agents endure, AI may become not an episode in a life but a presence within one.

The work ahead runs on both sides. With sociologists and psychologists, on the meaning side of displacement: what transition policies should measure beyond wages, and which alternative grounds for self-composition actually bear weight. And with the frontier AI labs, on the question their systems now raise: what an AI that stays should be to the person it accompanies, and by what standard the relationship counts as having gone well. URNAVA does not arrive at that question empty-handed: its candidate is on this page, by name, built to be tested.

02Ontological Layer

Existence, in practice

Clinical medicine · Bioengineering · Bioethics

The ontology already runs on clinical ground: sleep, anesthesia, dementia, and hypothermic arrest are among its test cases. As medicine and neurotechnology move toward the substrate itself, the question of what counts as the same being stops being academic and starts appearing on consent forms.

The work ahead is conceptual research with clinicians, neuroscientists, bioethicists, and the engineers reaching into the substrate through gene therapy and neural interfaces. Further out wait copying, pausing, restoring, porting: forks where two outcomes look physically identical and are ontologically opposite, the continuation of a being or a new one wearing its memories. The task is the same at every distance: the distinctions arrive before the procedures do, and agreement is built in advance rather than improvised at the bedside.

03Teleological Layer

Purpose, in practice

Frontier engineering · Education · Immersive worlds

The teleological problem does not wait for total abundance. It appears whenever inherited necessity recedes and possibility expands faster than orientation. The danger is not a lack of available goals, but an excess of them without sufficient grounds for choosing: more possible lives, longer horizons, and fewer external demands capable of deciding what a life is for. As constraints become variable, purpose can no longer be borrowed from the conditions that once imposed it; it has to be consciously formed and sustained by the subject.

The work ahead is to turn the teleology into testable constructs and usable material across those settings. With engineers of expansion, on space and multi-planetary programs, where the pull of what exceeds present reach already organizes real work. With educators, on what to teach when skills stop being scarce and the formation of ends matters as much as the acquisition of means. And with the makers of generated worlds, where expanding possibility reaches experience itself and the question becomes immediate: what makes an experience worth having when almost any experience can be produced? The teleology brings its proposal to each of these fields in advance. Not consolation for the transition, but equipment for it.

From the Author

I was born in 2000. The transitions described on this site are not my research topic; they are the years I will actually live. The need to face them arrived on its own, without assignment or occasion, and it has not left.

I continue to carry these questions forward on my own: stating positions, fixing conditions, and registering objections. I know what work done alone can establish, and what it cannot. A framework built in one room cannot test itself. It has to meet the fields where these problems will actually be decided, and it has to be willing to be corrected there.

So this page ends with a request rather than an offer. If research moves on any of these fronts (a lab, a studio, a clinic, a policy pilot, a classroom), I am asking plainly for the chance to take part, in whatever capacity is useful. I have no institution behind me and no credential to lead with. What I have is the work itself, and the seriousness of someone who does not intend to spend a lifetime beside these questions without ever touching them. The door below is open, and I will answer.

— GeonWoo Kim
